# Contributing terminology proposals

Anyone may propose an entry, correction, counterexample, or source through a repository issue or pull request. Plain text is sufficient. You do not need a trademark symbol, a special keyboard, a completed historical date, or a new replacement name to participate.

Use `Descriptive name ("Historical name", est. YYYY)` when a sourced historical annotation is useful. `Descriptive name ("Historical name"™, est. YYYY)` is also acceptable; the ASCII `(TM)` fallback may be used as well. **TM styling is optional and never a conformance requirement.** It does not identify a separate concept or attest legal status. Do not add a copyright symbol as a substitute for attribution.

If a date is unknown, say so. Specify whether a date concerns a publication, the term's first documented use, or another event. Do not infer a term's coinage date from the publication that later became associated with it.

## Proposal template

- Current term, language, domain, intended reader, and task.
- What the concept is, with a close alternative it must not be confused with.
- What the name says and which information must be learned externally.
- Why the name arose, with sources or an explicit unknown.
- Whether its historical or community identity remains useful.
- Proposed treatment: retain, supplement, alias, rename, structurally rename, disambiguate, split, historical-only, or preserve symbol/change expansion.
- Candidate descriptor, if useful, and what would be lost by using it.
- Alias policy: exact equivalent, broader topic, historical pointer, or another specified relation.
- Supporting sources, counterexamples, and evidence status.

A short initial proposal can enter discussion before all fields are complete. A published registry entry must expose unresolved fields rather than silently fill them. For a terminology family, show the relevant dimensions and at least one near-neighbor test.

## Review and correction

Author-generated examples are proposals. Semantic review, empirical testing, and adoption are separate statuses. A source for a conventional definition is not evidence that a proposed new name improves learning. Keep both positive and negative findings.

Do not rename community identities by majority vote among unrelated contributors. Record who uses a designation, what authority the review covers, and whether a descriptor is merely an orientation. Preserve source-language terms where a translation is contested. Do not upload private conversations or restricted cultural knowledge as supporting material.

Stable concept IDs survive display-name changes. Preserve old search labels with sense qualifiers. Do not perform global replacements across quotations or historical titles. Correct errors through a documented revision rather than quietly rewriting the earlier release.

Contributions to the manuscript and terminology data are offered under CC BY 4.0, consistent with the project's text license.
