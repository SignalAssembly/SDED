# Historical recovery and limits

Recovery date: September 8, 2026.

## Verified publication identity

DataCite's authoritative registration records identify Brian Wijaya as the author of **SDED™ Protocol Specification: Substantive-Description Eponym Deprecation — A Community Standard for Non-Arbitrary Naming in Knowledge Bases**, version v1.1, issued October 22, 2025.

- Version DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17418489
- Version-series DOI: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17418488
- Metadata: https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.5281/zenodo.17418489

The registration abstract establishes descriptive-first naming, phenomenological descriptors, epistemic transparency, pedagogy, database and ontology integration, community participation, and possible RFC-style revisions. The version DOI has an IsVersionOf relation to the series DOI. A related-identifier search returned this v1.1 version; that result is not proof that no unindexed draft exists.

Zenodo's public record API eventually returned the record and file metadata, confirming deposited v1.0 and v1.1 PDFs (4,199 and 6,758 bytes). The website, login page, and PDF download routes returned timeouts, including browser 504 responses. The deposited v1.1 PDF itself has therefore not yet been compared byte-for-byte with repository artifacts. Claims about the deposited v1.1 artifact are limited to verified metadata; the recovered founding transcript supplies separately identified drafting evidence. No v1.2 PDF is silently treated as the deposited v1.1 paper.

## Reconstructed chronology

- **October 15, 2025:** direct author preference for verbose category words in filenames to distinguish category from instance, a prepublication engineering antecedent.
- **October 22, 2025:** v1.1 published according to DOI registration. At 12:19, the author explicitly formulates Substantive-Description Eponym Deprecation. Earlier that morning he moves the proposed mark from the descriptive name to the historical name. At 13:29 he requests ASCII (TM) acceptance in v1.1, and at 13:36 adds propagation/reference notes. One later draft expands SDED as Standard Descriptive Eponym Dictionary; that inconsistency is not adopted as a rename.
- **October 23-27, 2025:** v1.2 development. Approved Vocabulary Set 1; surname-first scope; syntactic substitution; hierarchical punctuation; descriptive derivative subdomains; retention of attribution; repeated correction of overly generic or arbitrary replacements. The later branch adds Minimal Intervention, Sufficiently Substantive Descriptor, and Intermediate-Level Abbreviations rules.
- **Within the saved October excerpt:** the author rejects added words such as Framework or Theory where they change the concept's type; insists on retaining informative head nouns; rejects replacing a named fixed-point theorem with an undifferentiated Fixed-Point Theorem; permits more than five words when distinction requires it; then qualifies acronym expansion by the target reader's intermediate knowledge. Later expansion beyond surnames is anticipated, rather than prohibited forever.
- **February 9, 2026:** a session summary applies SDED to operational tool vocabulary; this supports continuity of the broader naming ambition, with weaker authority than direct author turns.
- **September 8, 2026:** music gives the method a new calibration case. The author progresses from shell formulas to quality/function separation, then to systematic triad-quality × seventh-quality naming, then to verbosity as recoverability. The present request adds multiple naming layers and explicit tests of cultural/historical value.

## Principles versus flawed examples

The author's demand for distinguishing descriptors is stronger than several generated examples. The branch still contains misleading or underdetermined replacements. In particular, **Continuous-Map Fixed-Point Theorem** omits the distinguishing domain assumptions; **Power Series** does not identify a Taylor series specifically; **Energy-Function Stability** risks naming a proof method rather than the stability property. These are reasons to test semantic equivalence, not reasons to discard descriptive naming.

The old implementation's line-length-triggered taxonomy and example split into heuristic versus deterministic algorithms also need revision: layout must not determine ontology, and those properties are not mutually exclusive. The new protocol separates display grouping, conceptual subsumption, and historical derivation.
